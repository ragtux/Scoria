# Rhyolite

Rhyolite is the volcanic equivalent of granite: more than 69 % SiO₂, low in iron and magnesium, and with a [[Magma viscosity|viscosity]] so high that it rarely flows far. It erupts as domes, as thick stubby flows, and, when gas-rich, as the [[Pumice]] and ash of Plinian eruptions.

## Three rocks from one melt

The same rhyolitic magma ends up as three different rocks depending on gas and cooling:

```
          gas-rich, quenched            →  Pumice
rhyolite  gas-poor, quenched            →  Obsidian
  melt    gas-poor, cooled slowly       →  Rhyolite (crystalline)
```

- [[Pumice]] keeps its bubbles.
- [[Obsidian]] lost them and froze to glass.
- Rhyolite proper crystallised, often with a flow-banded or spherulitic texture.

## Texture

Rhyolite lavas are usually porphyritic, with quartz and feldspar phenocrysts in a groundmass too fine to resolve. Flow banding, alternating layers of glassy and crystalline material, records the shearing of the viscous flow. Spherulites, radiating fibres of feldspar and cristobalite, form where glass has [[Glossary#^devitrification|devitrified]].
