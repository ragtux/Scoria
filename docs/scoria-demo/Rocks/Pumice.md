# Pumice

Pumice is the pale, frothy counterpart to [[Scoria]]: a silicic volcanic glass so full of tiny vesicles that it floats. Where scoria comes out of a basaltic vent in clots, pumice comes out of a rhyolitic or dacitic one as foam.

![[pumice-st-helens.jpg|420]]
*Dacitic pumice from the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens. Public domain.*

## Why it floats

A rhyolitic melt has a [[Magma viscosity|viscosity]] many thousand times that of basalt, so when it decompresses the bubbles nucleate in huge numbers but cannot grow or coalesce before the melt quenches. The result is a closed foam with vesicles a fraction of a millimetre across and a bulk density of 0.3 to 0.9 g/cm³. Pumice stays afloat until water works into the vesicles, which can take months; pumice rafts after submarine eruptions have crossed oceans.

> [!warning] Pumice rafts
> After the 2012 Havre eruption in the Kermadec arc, a raft of pumice covering about 400 km² drifted across the south-west Pacific for more than a year. Floating pumice is a shipping hazard and a seed-dispersal mechanism at the same time.

## Composition

| | Pumice | [[Scoria]] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SiO₂ | 65–75 % | 45–55 % |
| Colour | white to grey | black to red |
| Vesicle size | < 1 mm | 1–10 mm |
| Bulk density | 0.3–0.9 g/cm³ | 1.0–1.8 g/cm³ |
| Floats | yes | no |
| Eruption style | Plinian | [[Strombolian eruptions|Strombolian]] |

The same magma that makes pumice makes [[Obsidian]] when it loses its gas before it quenches, and [[Rhyolite]] when it crystallises.

## Uses

Pumice is the original abrasive: pumice stone for skin, pumice powder for polishing, and the "stone" in stone-washed denim. Ground pumice is a pozzolan and has been mixed into concrete since the Romans used it in the dome of the Pantheon.

## Tube pumice

Some pumice has vesicles stretched into parallel tubes, where the foam was sheared in the conduit as it rose. It splits along the tubes like wood along the grain and is a useful indicator of flow direction in a deposit.
