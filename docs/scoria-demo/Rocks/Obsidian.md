# Obsidian

Obsidian is natural glass: a [[Rhyolite|rhyolitic]] melt that lost its gas and cooled too fast for crystals to grow. It is black, breaks with a conchoidal fracture into edges sharper than steel, and is the rock this vault's software is named after.

![[obsidian.jpg|480]]
*Obsidian showing conchoidal fracture. Photo: James St. John, CC BY 2.0.*

## Why it is glass

A rhyolitic melt is so polymerised that atoms cannot rearrange into crystal lattices in the time a flow takes to cool. Dissolved water lowers the [[Magma viscosity|viscosity]] and lets crystals grow, so obsidian is always dry: under 1 % water. The same melt with its water still in it would have erupted as [[Pumice]].

## Ageing

Obsidian does not last. It absorbs water from the air and turns to perlite from the surface inward, and it slowly [[Glossary#^devitrification|devitrifies]] to a fine crystalline rock. Almost no obsidian older than about 20 million years survives, which is why it is a young volcanic rock by definition.

> [!tip] Obsidian hydration dating
> The hydration rind grows at a known rate, so measuring its thickness under the microscope dates when a surface was last broken. Archaeologists use it to date obsidian tools.

## Uses

The sharpest edge that can be made by hand: Mesoamerican blades, surgical scalpels in a few modern practices, and every arrowhead found near an obsidian source. The Ancient Greek *opsianos* lithos, after Obsius who supposedly found it in Ethiopia, is the origin of the name.
