# Reticulite

Reticulite is basaltic foam taken to its limit: a network of glass threads around polyhedral vesicles, with a vesicularity of 95 to 98 %. It is the least dense rock known, lighter than [[Pumice]], and yet it sinks, because every vesicle is open to its neighbours and water floods it at once.

It forms in the highest Hawaiian lava fountains, where clots of melt are stretched and inflated for several seconds before they land. [[Kīlauea]]'s 1959 Kīlauea Iki fountains, which reached 580 m, left a reticulite blanket downwind of the vent.

## Structure

The vesicle walls have drained into the junctions between bubbles, leaving a honeycomb of threads 10 to 50 µm thick. Under a hand lens it looks like a sponge; under the microscope like a soap foam frozen at the moment before it bursts.

> [!tip] Handling it
> Reticulite crumbles under a fingertip. Carry samples in a rigid box with no padding that can press on them, and never in a sample bag with [[Scoria]].

## Relation to scoria

| Vesicularity | Rock |
| --: | --- |
| 0–30 % | vesicular [[Basalt]] |
| 30–70 % | [[Scoria]] |
| 70–95 % | very vesicular scoria |
| > 95 % | reticulite |

It is a continuum: the same melt, more time in the air. See [[Vesiculation]].
