# Tuff

Tuff is consolidated volcanic ash: [[Tephra]] finer than 2 mm that has been compacted or cemented into rock. It can be any composition, but most tuff is rhyolitic to dacitic because those are the magmas that produce the most ash.

## Kinds

- **Air-fall tuff** — ash that settled from an eruption cloud, well sorted and bedded, thinning away from the vent.
- **Ignimbrite** — the deposit of a pyroclastic density current: poorly sorted ash and [[Pumice]], often welded where it was emplaced hot.
- **Welded tuff** — an ignimbrite whose glass shards and pumice were flattened and fused by their own heat into a dense, streaky rock called *eutaxitic*.
- **Scoria tuff** — mafic ash and lapilli near a [[Cinder cone]], cemented by palagonite where it was wet.

## Reading a welded tuff

| Zone | Where | Texture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| non-welded base | bottom, chilled against the ground | loose, pumice intact |
| densely welded | middle | black glass, flattened pumice (*fiamme*) |
| partly welded top | top, cooled by air | pink, vapour-phase crystals |

> [!note]
> The Bishop Tuff in California, erupted from Long Valley 767 000 years ago, is 200 m thick near the caldera and shows the whole sequence in one cliff.

## Named tuffs

Roman *tufo* was quarried for building stone around Naples and Rome, and the Yellow Tuff of Naples is a single ignimbrite from the Campi Flegrei. Tuff rings and tuff cones, which form where magma meets water, are the wet cousins of the dry [[Cinder cone]].
