# Andesite

Andesite is the intermediate volcanic rock, 52 to 63 % SiO₂, named after the Andes and typical of subduction-zone volcanoes everywhere. It is more viscous than [[Basalt]] and less than [[Rhyolite]], and its eruptions are correspondingly in between: lava flows that are thick and blocky, and explosions that are serious without being catastrophic.

![[paricutin-basaltic-andesite.jpg|420]]
*Basaltic andesite from [[Parícutin]], in the Smithsonian's collection. CC0.*

## Basaltic andesite

The boundary with basalt at 52 % SiO₂ is a line on the [[TAS classification|TAS diagram]], not a change in the rock. Parícutin's lava and [[Scoria]] are basaltic andesite at 55 to 60 % SiO₂, and over its nine years the eruption moved steadily up that range as the magma chamber fractionated.

| Year | SiO₂ (%) | Note |
| --: | --: | --- |
| 1943 | 55.0 | cone building, scoria |
| 1945 | 56.2 | flows bury Parangaricutiro |
| 1948 | 58.1 | |
| 1952 | 60.3 | final flows |

> [!note] Why the trend matters
> Nine years of samples from one monogenetic vent is a record of a magma body evolving in real time. It is one of the reasons Parícutin is in every petrology textbook.

## Minerals

Plagioclase is always present; the mafic mineral is pyroxene in dry andesites and hornblende where the magma was wet. Hornblende andesites are the ones that make domes and pyroclastic flows.
