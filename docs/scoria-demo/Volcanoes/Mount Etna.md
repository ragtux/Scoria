# Mount Etna

Europe's tallest and most active volcano, a stratovolcano on the east coast of Sicily built of [[Basalt|basaltic]] and trachybasaltic lava flows and [[Scoria]]. Its summit craters are in near-constant [[Strombolian eruptions|Strombolian]] activity, and every few years it produces *paroxysms*: hours-long lava fountains that rain lapilli on the towns below.

## The 2021 paroxysms

From 16 February 2021 the South-East Crater produced more than fifty paroxysms in a year. Each one followed the same script: Strombolian bursts for a few hours, a fountain to 1 km or more, a tephra column to 10 km, and a lava flow down the Valle del Bove. Lapilli fell on Catania on the first day and closed the airport.

![[etna-lapillus.jpg|480]]
*A lapillus from the 16 February 2021 fall, picked up in Catania. Fresh scoria: black, glassy walls, millimetre vesicles. Photo: James St. John, CC BY 2.0.*

> [!info] Lapilli, not ash
> The 2021 falls near the volcano were mostly lapilli, 2 to 64 mm, because the fountains were high and the clasts were large. Ash of the same eruptions reached the Peloponnese. See [[Tephra]] for the size classes.

## Why scoria

Etna's magma is gas-rich and only moderately viscous, which is the recipe for scoria: enough gas to fragment the melt, not enough viscosity to hold a foam. The clasts quench in the air, land hot, and build the cone around the active vent. The South-East Crater grew 100 m in the 2021 sequence alone.
