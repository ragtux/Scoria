# Kīlauea

A shield volcano on the island of Hawaiʻi, and the type locality for Hawaiian-style eruptions: lava fountains, lakes and long pāhoehoe flows of very fluid [[Basalt]]. Where [[Mount Etna]] and [[Stromboli]] make [[Scoria]] from bursting gas slugs, Kīlauea makes it from fountains, and at the top of the highest fountains makes [[Reticulite]] and Pele's hair.

## Fountains

| Eruption | Fountain height | Product |
| --- | --: | --- |
| Kīlauea Iki, 1959 | 580 m | reticulite, Puʻu Puaʻi cone |
| Mauna Ulu, 1969 | 540 m | spatter, scoria |
| Puʻu ʻŌʻō, 1983–86 | 470 m | the Puʻu ʻŌʻō cone |
| Fissure 8, 2018 | 80 m | spatter cone, 35 km of lava |

The clasts in a fountain spend one to ten seconds in the air. Spatter lands molten and welds; [[Scoria]] lands plastic and stays loose; [[Reticulite]] forms where the clasts inflated for longest.

> [!note] Pele's hair and Pele's tears
> Threads of basaltic glass drawn out from fountain droplets, and the droplets themselves, named after the Hawaiian volcano goddess. They are the fine end of the same fragmentation that makes scoria at the coarse end.

## Flows

Kīlauea's flows are pāhoehoe near the vent and ʻaʻā further out, as the lava cools and its [[Magma viscosity|viscosity]] rises. Pāhoehoe flows form tubes, which is how the 1983–2018 Puʻu ʻŌʻō eruption carried lava 12 km to the sea for decades.
