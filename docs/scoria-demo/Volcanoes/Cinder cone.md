# Cinder cone

The simplest volcano: a cone of loose [[Scoria]] piled around a vent at the angle of repose, with a bowl-shaped crater at the top. Most are [[Glossary#^monogenetic|monogenetic]], built in a single eruption lasting months to a few years, and most are small, 30 to 400 m high. There are tens of thousands of them.

![[cinder-cone-section.svg|620]]
*Cross-section of a cinder cone with a basal lava flow.*

## Anatomy

| Part | Made of | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cone | bedded [[Scoria]] lapilli and bombs | slopes at the angle of repose, 30–33° |
| crater | the same, collapsed inward | width about 40 % of the base diameter |
| rim | welded spatter and oxidised scoria | the red at [[Sunset Crater]] |
| lava flow | [[Basalt]] or [[Andesite]] | breaks out of the base, not the crater, because the cone is too weak to hold it |
| tephra blanket | ash and lapilli | thinning downwind |

## How it grows

1. Fissure opens; a curtain of fire fountains along it.
2. The vent localises. [[Strombolian eruptions|Strombolian]] bursts throw scoria that lands within a few hundred metres.
3. The pile grows until its slopes reach the angle of repose, then grows upward only.
4. Lava, denser than the cone, breaks out at the base and rafts pieces of the cone away.
5. The eruption wanes and stops. The cone is finished; it will never erupt again.

[[Parícutin]] did all five in nine years and was watched doing it.

## Ageing

A fresh cone is sharp-rimmed and bare. Erosion rounds the rim, gullies the flanks and lowers the slope; plants take hold. In the San Francisco Volcanic Field the progression from [[Sunset Crater]] (900 years) to the pine-covered cones beside it (hundreds of thousands of years) is visible in a single photograph:

![[cinder-cones-arizona.jpg|520]]

> [!tip] Dating by shape
> Height-to-width ratio and maximum slope angle decrease steadily with age, so a cone's profile gives a rough date even before anyone has dated its lava.

## Not to be confused with

- **Spatter cone** — built of clots that landed molten and welded; steeper and smaller.
- **Tuff cone** — magma met water; fine ash, wide low profile. See [[Tuff]].
- **Stratovolcano** — many eruptions over many thousands of years, layered lava and tephra, e.g. [[Mount Etna]].
