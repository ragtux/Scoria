# Sunset Crater

The youngest [[Cinder cone]] in the San Francisco Volcanic Field of northern Arizona, and the reason the field is red. It erupted once, around 1085 CE, built a cone 340 m high, and sent the Bonito and Kana-a lava flows out from its base. The Sinagua people who farmed the area moved out during the eruption and back in afterwards, onto soil improved by the ash.

![[sunset-crater.jpg|600]]
*The cone from the north-west. The rim is oxidised red and yellow scoria, which gave the crater its name. Photo: Nhartmannphotos, CC BY 4.0.*

## The eruption

1. A fissure opened and a curtain of fire threw [[Scoria]] along its length.
2. Activity focused at one vent; [[Strombolian eruptions|Strombolian]] bursts built the cone at the angle of repose.
3. Lava broke out of the cone's base: the Bonito flow to the north-west, the Kana-a flow to the north-east.
4. A final burst of fountaining oxidised the rim to the sunset colours.
5. Ash and lapilli fell over 2 000 km², a metre deep near the cone.

> [!warning] Closed to climbing
> The cone has been closed to hikers since 1973, after foot traffic cut gullies into the loose scoria that will not heal in a human lifetime. The Lava Flow Trail at the base is the way to see it.

## The neighbours

![[cinder-cones-arizona.jpg|600]]
*Older cinder cones east of Sunset Crater, with pine growing on them. Photo: James St. John, CC BY 2.0.*

The field holds some 600 cones spanning six million years. The older ones are green with ponderosa pine and rounded by erosion; Sunset Crater is bare and sharp. Cone profile is a clock: see [[Cinder cone#Ageing]].
