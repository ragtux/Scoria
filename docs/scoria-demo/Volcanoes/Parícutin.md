# Parícutin

On 20 February 1943 a fissure opened in Dionisio Pulido's cornfield in Michoacán, Mexico. By the next morning there was a cone 50 m high; by the end of the year, 336 m. It erupted for nine years and seven days, buried two villages under lava, and became the first volcano whose whole life was watched by geologists.

## What it taught

- A [[Cinder cone]] is built in weeks and finished in years: 90 % of the height came in the first year.
- [[Glossary#^monogenetic|Monogenetic]] vents are fed by a single batch of magma, which can be watched fractionating. The lava went from 55 to 60 % SiO₂ over nine years, see [[Andesite#Basaltic andesite]].
- Tephra volume (about 1.3 km³) exceeded lava volume (about 0.7 km³), the usual ratio for Strombolian cones.
- The eruption rate decayed roughly exponentially, a pattern since found at other monogenetic vents.

> [!quote] Dionisio Pulido, 20 February 1943
> "I saw how, in the hole, the ground swelled and raised itself 2 or 2.5 metres high, and a kind of smoke or fine dust, grey like ashes, began to rise up…" (recorded by Foshag and González-Reyna, 1956)

## Today

The cone stands in a field of its own lava, with the towers of the San Juan church rising out of the ʻaʻā. It will not erupt again; the next vent in the Michoacán–Guanajuato field will open somewhere else, as it has 1 400 times before.
