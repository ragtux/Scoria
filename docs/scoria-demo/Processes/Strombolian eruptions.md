# Strombolian eruptions

The eruption style that makes scoria. Discrete bursts, seconds apart to an hour apart, each throwing incandescent clots of basaltic melt a few tens to hundreds of metres. The clots land as [[Scoria]] and bombs and build a [[Cinder cone]] around the vent.

![[Glossary#^strombolian]]

## The slug

Gas bubbles in a low-viscosity magma rise faster than the magma itself. They catch up with each other and coalesce until a single bubble, a *slug*, fills the conduit. The slug pushes a film of melt ahead of it; when it reaches the surface the film bursts and the gas vents in one pulse. Infrasound records the pulse; the clots in the air are the scoria.

## Where it sits

| Style | Gas state | Viscosity | Products |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hawaiian | bubbly flow, fountains | lowest | spatter, [[Reticulite]], Pele's hair |
| **Strombolian** | slugs | low | [[Scoria]], bombs |
| Vulcanian | pressurised plug | moderate | blocks, ash |
| Plinian | fragmentation in the conduit | high | [[Pumice]], ash columns |

[[Kīlauea]] is Hawaiian, [[Stromboli]] and [[Mount Etna]] are Strombolian with excursions to the next row up, and the eruptions that make [[Tuff]] are the bottom two.

> [!tip] Telling the deposits apart
> A Strombolian deposit is coarse, poorly sorted close to the vent, and made of clasts that were hot enough to deform on landing. A Hawaiian deposit is welded near the vent. A Plinian deposit is well sorted, widespread and pale.

## Paroxysms

The regular Strombolian regime can escalate when a large batch of gas-rich magma arrives from depth: fountains to a kilometre, a tephra column, lapilli fall tens of kilometres away. [[Mount Etna#The 2021 paroxysms]] is the recent reference case; [[Stromboli#Paroxysms]] the dangerous one.
