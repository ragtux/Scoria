# Tephra

![[Glossary#^tephra]]

Tephra is classified by grain size first and composition second, because size is what controls where it lands and what hazard it poses.

## By size

| Name | Size | Lands | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ash | < 2 mm | hundreds to thousands of km | the 2021 [[Mount Etna|Etna]] ash in Greece |
| lapilli | 2–64 mm | kilometres | [[Scoria]] on a [[Cinder cone]] |
| blocks | > 64 mm, solid when thrown | hundreds of metres | conduit wall rock |
| bombs | > 64 mm, molten when thrown | hundreds of metres | spindle and bread-crust bombs |

![[lapilli-ascension.jpg|480]]
*Basaltic lapilli from Ascension Island, every clast a piece of [[Scoria]]. Photo: James St. John, CC BY 2.0.*

## Bombs

Bombs are shaped in flight and on landing:

- **Spindle** — twisted into a fusiform shape by spinning in the air.
- **Bread-crust** — a quenched rind cracked by the still-vesiculating interior.
- **Cow-pat** — landed too fluid to keep any shape.
- **Ribbon** — a thread of melt that stayed in one piece.

A bomb from [[Kīlauea]], still glowing where it landed, is in [[Kīlauea#Bombs]].

## By composition

The same size scheme applies whatever the magma, but the products differ:

- Mafic tephra is [[Scoria]] and dense glass; its ash is dark.
- Silicic tephra is [[Pumice]] and glass shards; its ash is pale and goes much further, because silicic eruptions are more explosive.

## What it becomes

Tephra that stays loose is a deposit; tephra that compacts or cements is [[Tuff]]. Welded tephra, fused by its own heat, is ignimbrite.

## Hazards

- [ ] Roof collapse under ash more than about 10 cm thick, worse when wet
- [ ] Aircraft engine damage from ash at any concentration
- [ ] Ballistic bombs within about 2 km of a Strombolian vent
- [x] Lapilli fall on Catania, February 2021: closed the airport, broke no roofs
